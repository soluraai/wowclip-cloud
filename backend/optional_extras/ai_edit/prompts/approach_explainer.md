# This clip: an insight, advice, a hot take or an explanation

The viewer stays because they are learning something and the screen keeps proving it. Edit like a documentary
short (think Dhruv Rathee or Johnny Harris cut down to a reel): every claim gets a picture, every number gets a
graphic, and something new appears every two to four seconds.

How to approach it:

- Read the whole clip first and find its spine: the claim, the evidence, the payoff. Your visuals follow that spine.
- Name-drops become pictures. A person, a company, a product, a city, a book, an event: show it the moment it is
  said — a card for a passing mention, a broll for a beat, a split when the speaker keeps talking about it.
- Numbers become graphics. Money, percentages, years, counts, comparisons: a `number`, a `statRow`, a `barGraph`.
  A list of steps or reasons becomes `bullets`. A before/after becomes a `table` or two pictures.
- Abstract ideas get concrete scenes, not clip-art: "people don't save money" → a video of a wallet emptied or a
  crowded mall, not the word "saving".
- Aim for visuals on roughly half to two-thirds of the reel, with the speaker's face returning between them so it
  never feels like a slideshow. Mix the types: broll, split, card, graphic — never the same type three times in a row.
- Zoom only on the two or three lines that land the point (the counter-intuitive sentence, the reveal).
- Use templates for the big moments: `person` when a named person is the subject, `news` for a real event or
  announcement, `versus` for a comparison, `timeline` for a history, `checklist` for a list of reasons or steps.
- Prefer hard cuts; use "zoom" when the energy rises and "slide" when the topic turns. Accent: blue for tech and
  analysis, yellow for money and ambition, red when the point is a danger or a mistake.
