# This clip: a story

The viewer stays to find out what happened. Edit like a story-driven documentary: show the world of the story —
the places, the people, the objects, the moment — so the listener can picture it, and keep the speaker's face for
the turns of the story and the ending.

How to approach it:

- Read the whole clip first and mark the beats: setup, the turn, the payoff. Visuals carry the setup and the middle;
  the turn and the payoff belong to the face.
- Illustrate the concrete details the speaker gives: a place ("a tiny flat in Delhi"), an object ("my first
  laptop"), a time ("2008"), a person. Atmospheric `video` broll works well for places and moods; `photo` for named
  people, companies and events.
- A year or a date can become a quick `graphic` with a `kicker` and a `number`, or a `card`.
- Use `split` when the speaker describes a scene for a while, so the picture stays up while they talk.

- Transitions: hard cuts, "fade" for a reflective moment, "slide" when the story jumps in time or place.
  Accent: yellow for warmth and triumph, blue for calm, red for conflict.
- Templates fit stories well: `timeline` when the story moves through years, `person` when someone new enters
  the story, `headline` for the one line the story builds to.
